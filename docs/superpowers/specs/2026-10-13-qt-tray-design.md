# Qt tray icon and macOS About panel (design)

Status: approved to go ahead by Nick, 2026-10-13, with the open questions at their proposed answers. A Codex review (gpt-6-sol, high) the same day found five defects; they are folded in and listed in the review section at the end. The choices marked "proposed" are mine and are the ones to check. Written against `qt-updater-113`, first at 61af808 (main plus #113 slices 1 and 2) and then at ccb700a (#113 slice 3), whose `run_qt` wiring decision 6 builds on.

## Goal

Qt mode (`TOKITTY_QT=1`) gets a tray icon through `QSystemTrayIcon`, with the same menu, toggle, refresh, notification and handover behaviour as Tk's `TrayManager` (`tokitty/tray.py`), and the macOS About panel that Tk has had since #98. On macOS this brings back the menu-bar item that #45 took away from Tk, because Qt owns the main thread there and pystray's off-thread `-[NSApplication run]` is not involved. After this issue the only Qt gaps left are packaging (#115) and the Tk removal (#142).

## Scope and non-goals

In scope: a Qt tray class, its wiring in `run_qt`, the `QtHost` and `QtUpdateHost` seams it fills, the About item in the macOS app menu, a toolkit-free home for the tray icon's sprite choice, the self-check rule for `pystray`, and the isolation tests that keep pystray and pyobjc out of `tokitty/qt`.

Not in scope: any change to the Tk tray or the Tk About panel; removing `pystray` or `pyobjc` from the dependencies or the frozen build (decision 1); an About item in the tray or right-click menu, which Tk does not have; macOS notifications (decision 5); updating the tray icon when the cat's colours change, which Tk does not do either; replacing Pillow (#115).

## Proposed decisions

1. **`pystray` stays a dependency until #142.** The issue says #114 drops it, but Tk mode is still the default, its tray is pystray (`tray.py:43-71`), and the Tk path does not change in this series. Removing `pystray` from `pyproject.toml:8` would leave every Tk install without a tray, and taking it out of the frozen build would also take `pyobjc`, which the Tk About panel imports as `AppKit` on macOS (`ui.py:850-858`). So #114 makes pystray unused in Qt mode and enforces that with a test (decision 9), and the dependency goes in #142 together with `tokitty/tray.py`. With Nick's OK, a comment on #142 records the move so the line in #114's body is not lost.

2. **`QtTray`, a new class with `TrayManager`'s public surface.** `tokitty/qt/tray.py` holds `QtTray(menu_provider, state_dir, colorway, pattern, *, on_action_done=None, icon_factory=None, tray_available=None, platform=sys.platform)`, with `available`, `start()`, `stop()`, `refresh()`, `notify(title, message) -> bool` and `set_enabled(enabled)`, matching the methods `run_gui`, `TkHost` and `announcer` call (`__main__.py:372-396, 523, 558`, `update_controller.py:142-158`, `update_check.py:201-205`). There is no thread: the icon lives on the GUI thread and its signals arrive there. The two injectables (the `QSystemTrayIcon` factory and the availability check) exist for tests, because the offscreen platform has no system tray at all (`QSystemTrayIcon.isSystemTrayAvailable()` is false there).
   - `available` is decided once, after the `QApplication` exists: `isSystemTrayAvailable()` and an icon image that builds without raising, as `TrayManager._probe` guards both the image and the backend (`tray.py:106-121`). There is no darwin exclusion: that is the point of the issue. Linux without a StatusNotifierItem host or an XEmbed tray (stock GNOME without the AppIndicator extension) reports false, so the app runs with no tray and the Settings row stays hidden, as it does when pystray fails today.
   - `start()` does nothing unless `available` and not already started. It builds the icon with the image, the tooltip "Tokitty" (pystray's title), and a context menu rendered from `menu_provider()`, then shows it. A failure sets `available` false and leaves no icon, as `TrayManager.start` does (`tray.py:123-134`). Clicking the icon does nothing beyond what the platform does with a context menu (right click on Windows and Linux, any click on macOS), as with pystray, which has no default item here.
   - `stop()` hides the icon and drops it. Hiding before exit matters on Windows, where an icon still registered when the process ends stays in the notification area until the pointer passes over it.
   - `set_enabled(enabled)` writes `tray_enabled` through `update_settings` and starts or stops, as `tray.py:189-194`.

3. **The tray menu is built once and updated in place, as pystray's is.** Pystray builds its menu once and re-reads each item's getters on `update_menu()` (`tray.py:145-164`); the Qt tray does the same, so no `QAction` is ever deleted while the app runs. Clearing and re-rendering on refresh (the first draft) can delete the action the user just picked: on macOS and on Linux's DBusMenu the native menu reports its close before Qt delivers the activation to the `QAction`, and there is no dependable cross-platform signal for "the native menu is closed now" to postpone a rebuild to.
   - `tokitty/qt/menu.py`'s `render_menu` gains a return value: a `sync()` callable that re-reads every rendered item's getters (text and visibility from `dynamic_label`, checked state from `checkbox` and `radio_selected`). A `dynamic_label` item is now always created, hidden while its getter returns no text, rather than skipped. The right-click menu, rebuilt on every open, ignores the return value and looks the same as today, since a hidden action is not shown. One renderer serves both menus, so nothing is copied.
   - `QtTray` renders its menu once in `start()` and calls `sync()` in `refresh()`. The model's structure is fixed for the life of the launch (the update item is in it from the start, hidden), so updating in place covers every change `refresh` exists for.
   - Each tray action still runs through `QTimer.singleShot(0, ...)` and then calls `on_action_done`, which is `TrayManager`'s `root.after(0, run)` (`tray.py:93-104`) on Qt, so the action never runs inside the native menu's own event handling.
   The menu is the model for pane 0, as in Tk (`__main__.py:372`). It is parentless and owned by `QtTray`, not registered with `QtHost`: it is a popup, not a window, and Always in front does not apply to it.

4. **The icon is the same sprite as Tk's, through a shared helper.** Tk draws frame 0 of the "content" state at `TRAY_ICON_SCALE` 2 with pane 0's colorway and pattern, padded to a square (`tray.py:25-40`, `__main__.py:371-373`). The frame choice and the scale move to a toolkit-free `tokitty/tray_icon.py` (`TRAY_ICON_SCALE`, `tray_raster(colorway, pattern) -> (width, height, rgba)`), which `tray._default_image_factory` calls and `tray.py` re-exports `TRAY_ICON_SCALE` from, so nothing is copied; the Tk tray's output is byte-identical. `QtTray` turns the raster into a square `QImage` (as `qt/kit.py:114-120` does for sprites) and a `QIcon`. On macOS the colour image goes into the menu bar as it is, not as a template image; whether it reads well at menu-bar size is a manual check.

5. **`notify` returns True when the message was submitted to Qt, and is always False on macOS.** `announcer` writes `notified_tag` only when `notify` returns True (`update_check.py:204-211`), so a False is safe (the release is announced again on the next run) and a True ends the announcing for that tag. True means "submitted", not "seen": neither Qt nor pystray can tell whether a toast appeared (Qt's documentation warns that a message may not be shown, for example when the desktop suppresses notifications), and Tk's contract is the same backend attempt (`tray.py:166-179`). The durable announcement is the update item at the top of the tray and right-click menus, which stays until the update is installed. On Windows and Linux, `notify` calls `showMessage(title, message)` and returns True when the icon is shown and `supportsMessages()` is true; an exception returns False. On macOS it returns False without calling `showMessage`. Qt 6.12's Cocoa tray still posts through the deprecated `NSUserNotificationCenter`, which current macOS may drop without a word for an app that has no notification permission, and that would be a True for a toast nobody saw. Nothing can be tried until #115 makes a Qt Mac bundle, so enabling it is a follow-up for #115's checklist.

6. **`run_qt` wires the tray where `run_gui` does, in a stated order.** #113 slice 3 (ccb700a) builds `QtUpdateHost`, the controller and the checker right after `host.attach(controls)`, before the panes get their labels and before the Surprise me randomizing. The tray needs the randomized colours (Tk reads `pane0._colorway` after randomizing, `__main__.py:366-373`), and `QtUpdateHost` needs the tray, so `_run_locked` runs in this order: `host.attach(controls)`, the pane labels, the randomizing, then the tray, then `QtUpdateHost`, the controller, the checker and the update seams. The update block moves down as one piece; nothing in it reads anything the moved-over lines set. The tray starts at the existing late startup point. Then:
   - `tray = QtTray(lambda: menu_model(0), state_dir, colorway=..., pattern=...)` from pane 0's customization, with `on_action_done=host.notify_state_changed`.
   - `QtHost` gains `tray_refresh`, held as `None` like its other seams; `notify_state_changed` calls it before `settings_refresh`, which is `ui.notify_state_changed`'s order (`ui.py:890-897`), and its comment about there being no tray goes. The widget's `on_menu_action_done` is already `host.notify_state_changed`, so a change made from the right-click menu reaches the tray, which is what Tk's `window.on_menu_action_done = tray.refresh` (`__main__.py:380`) is for.
   - When `tray.available`, `host.tray_enabled` reads a plain `{"enabled": settings.tray_enabled}` and `host.on_toggle_tray` flips it and calls `tray.set_enabled`, as `__main__.py:381-389`. The General tab then shows the row with no change to `qt/settings.py` (it hides the row while either seam is `None`, `qt/settings.py:172-174`).
   - `make_onboarder(state_dir, usage_state, tray.refresh)` replaces the no-op at `qt/app.py:396`.
   - #113's `TrayStandIn` (`qt/app.py`, slice 3) keeps its `refresh`, which is `host.notify_state_changed` and now refreshes the tray too, and its `notify` delegates to `tray.notify` instead of returning False. Its docstring changes to say so. Nothing in `announcer` changes.
   - The tray starts after the pollers and before `app.exec()` when `tray.available and settings.tray_enabled`, as `__main__.py:523-524`, and the `finally` stops it first, guarded like the other stops, as `__main__.py:558`. The menu's Quit stays `app.quit`; the `finally` is what hides the icon.
   - The debug-state launch keeps no tray, as it keeps no updater.

7. **The handover host stops and restarts the tray as `TkHost` does.** `QtUpdateHost` takes `tray` and `tray_enabled` (keyword-only, required), and `run_qt` passes the `QtTray` and `lambda: load_settings(state_dir).tray_enabled`, as `TkHost` gets (`__main__.py:445-455`). `hide` calls `tray.stop()` first, before it hides the windows and releases the lock (`update_controller.py:142-148`), so the new copy's icon is never shown beside the old one's. `restore`, after the lock is reclaimed and the windows are shown again, starts the tray when `tray_enabled()` and then calls `tray.refresh()` (`:150-158`). A `restore` that raises because another copy holds the lock leaves the tray stopped, as Tk's does. #113's host tests pass a fake tray.

8. **The macOS About panel joins the Settings item in the app menu.** `make_preferences_menu` (`qt/app.py:47-62`) becomes `make_app_menu(open_settings, show_about)`, adding an "About Tokitty" action with `AboutRole` to the same menu, which Qt moves into the application menu as it moves the Settings action. `show_about` does what `ui.register_about_panel` does (`ui.py:843-867`): a frozen build shows the native panel, with `{"Version": ""}` so the version is not printed twice, and a source run shows "Tokitty <version>\nRunning from source." in an information box titled "About Tokitty", parented as `QtHost.warn` parents. The native call goes through `tokitty/qt/macos.py`'s ctypes `ObjC` rather than `AppKit`. arm64 passes arguments by the exact prototype and ctypes' default return type is `int`, which truncates a pointer, so every new shape is declared in full: `objc_getClass(c_char_p) -> c_void_p`; `(id, SEL) -> id` for `[NSApplication sharedApplication]`; `(id, SEL, c_char_p) -> id` for `[NSString stringWithUTF8String:]`; `(id, SEL, id, id) -> id` for `[NSDictionary dictionaryWithObject:forKey:]`; and `(id, SEL, id) -> void` for `orderFrontStandardAboutPanelWithOptions:`. It runs on the GUI thread, inside AppKit's event loop, whose autorelease pool covers the temporaries. A Python exception there prints one line and falls back to the information box; a wrong prototype would crash instead, which is why the native call is also run on Nick's Mac in this issue from a source launch (it opens Python's panel there), not only against a fake. The frozen panel's contents (Tokitty's name, version and copyright from its Info.plist) cannot be seen until #115 builds a Qt Mac bundle, so that check goes on #115's checklist. In a source run Qt names the app-menu items after the running bundle, which is Python's, so the item may read "About Python" there; the manual check records what it says rather than expecting "About Tokitty".

9. **The isolation test blocks pystray and pyobjc for Qt modules.** `tests/test_toolkit_isolation.py`'s Qt test blocks only tkinter today (`:12-15`, `:78-92`). It gains `pystray`, `AppKit`, `Foundation` and `objc` in its blocked list and in its leak check, `tokitty.qt.tray` joins `_QT_MODULES`, and `tokitty.tray_icon` joins `_MODULES`. `tokitty/qt/tray.py` does not import `tokitty.tray`.

10. **The self-check reports `pystray` exactly when the build declares `tk`.** `frozen.self_check` reports `pystray` unconditionally (`frozen.py:93-96, 158`). Every shipped updater requires `tk_root` and refuses any failed entry, but does not require `pystray` (`update_install.py:389-395`). A build without `tk` (#142's) will not carry pystray, so an unconditional entry would fail, and every update from v0.6.x to it would be refused. So `pystray` follows the `TOKITTY_BUILD_TOOLKITS` declaration from #113 decision 7, as `tkinter` does: present when it names `tk`, absent otherwise. A build made with the default, or with #115's `tk,qt`, reports exactly what it reports today. No Qt tray entry is added: a missing system tray is a supported state, not a broken bundle, and a check that needed one would fail on headless CI runners. This needs #113 slice 4 in the base.

Unmarked:

The existing `tests/test_tray.py` and `tests/test_tray_factory.py` run unchanged.
- `frozen.self_check`: `pystray` present with the default declaration and with `tk,qt`, absent with `qt` alone; `run_self_check` accepts each report.

Marked `qt`, offscreen, with a fake icon factory and availability check (no real tray exists offscreen):

- `QtTray`: unavailable when the check is false or the image raises, and then `start`, `refresh` and `notify` do nothing and `notify` returns False; `start` shows one icon with the tooltip and a square icon, and a second `start` builds nothing; `stop` hides it; `set_enabled` writes `settings.json` and starts or stops.
- Menu: built once from the provider; the update item exists but is hidden until the label getter returns text, and is visible with that text after `refresh`; a checkbox or radio change made elsewhere shows after `refresh`; `refresh` never creates or deletes a `QAction` (the same action objects before and after, including while the menu is shown); a tray action runs after the event loop turns, not inside `triggered`, and `on_action_done` follows it.
- `render_menu`: the right-click menu's visible items are unchanged with and without an update label (the existing `tests/qt` menu tests run unchanged), and the returned `sync()` updates text, visibility and checked state.
- `notify`: True after `showMessage` when shown and messages are supported (submitted, not seen); False when stopped, unsupported, raising, or on `platform="darwin"` (and `showMessage` is not called there). With `announcer`, `notified_tag` is written only after a True.
- `run_qt` wiring, in the `make_world` style #112 and #113 use: with Surprise me on, the tray's colours are pane 0's randomized ones and `QtUpdateHost` holds that same tray; the General tab shows the tray row only with an available tray, and toggling it writes `settings.json` and shows or hides the icon; a right-click menu action refreshes the tray; `tray.refresh` reaches the onboarder; the tray stops in the `finally`.
- `QtUpdateHost`: `hide` stops the tray before releasing the lock; `restore` starts it only when `tray_enabled()` and always refreshes; a `restore` that raises leaves it stopped.
- About: `make_app_menu` returns a Settings action with `PreferencesRole` and an About action with `AboutRole`; `show_about` in a source run shows the box with the version text; in a frozen run it calls the native panel through a fake `ObjC` with the right selectors and the empty Version option, and falls back to the box when the fake raises.

Manual, Windows (Cucumber, 100% and 150%), from source with `TOKITTY_QT=1` and a scratch `LOCALAPPDATA` only (the `C:\tmp\qt112-launch.ps1` pattern), never the real state dir: the icon appears with the cat and the tooltip; its menu matches the right-click menu, and a View or Always in front change made in one shows in the other; Settings opened from the tray comes up in front with Always in front on and off; the General toggle hides and shows the icon and survives a restart; with `TOKITTY_UPDATE_API_URL` at a local fake release feed, a toast appears (seen, checked separately from what is stored), `update-state.json` then holds the tag, and the next launch shows no toast but still shows the update item at the top of both menus; Quit leaves no icon behind in the notification area.

Manual, macOS (Nick's Mac), from source: the menu-bar item appears and is legible in light and dark menu bars; its menu opens and every item works; Settings opened from it comes to the front; the General toggle removes and restores it; the app menu's About item (named after the running bundle, so possibly "About Python") shows the source-run box; and the native panel call, run from a source Qt session with a one-line script, opens Python's About panel without crashing. Linux: no automated test reaches a real StatusNotifierItem host (offscreen and xvfb have none, and running a tray host under xvfb in CI is more machinery than this issue's risk on Linux warrants); with the in-place menu there is no close signal to depend on, so the remaining Linux risk is the menu host's own rendering. 

## Slices

1. Tray core: `tokitty/tray_icon.py` with the Tk tray on it, `tokitty/qt/tray.py`, the isolation changes, and their tests. Touches the Tk path only through the move, which lands with `tests/test_tray*.py` and the gui suite passing unchanged.
2. Wiring: `QtHost.tray_refresh`, `run_qt`, the announcer stand-in, the onboarder, `QtUpdateHost`'s tray, and their tests. Depends on 1 and on #113 slice 3.
3. macOS About: `make_app_menu`, `show_about`, the `ObjC` call shapes, and their tests. Independent of 1 and 2.
4. Self-check: the `pystray` rule and its tests. Depends on #113 slice 4.
5. Manual checks on Windows and the Mac, after 1 to 3. No committed code unless they find a defect.

Proposed: one PR. Its base is `main` if #113 has merged by then; otherwise #113 merges first and `main` is merged into this branch before the PR opens.

## Open questions

1. Is keeping `pystray` until #142 (decision 1) right, rather than making it an extra that only Tk installs pull in? An extra would change how Tk mode is installed, which is the Tk path changing.
2. Is a menu-bar item on by default on macOS right? `tray_enabled` defaults to true and has been ignored on darwin since #45, so every Mac user who runs Qt mode gets one on first launch. The alternative is a darwin-only default of false, which is a settings change Tk would then also read.
3. Should macOS notifications stay off (decision 5) until #115 can try them in a signed bundle, or should #114 try `showMessage` from a source run on the Mac first?

## Review (Codex, 2026-10-13)

gpt-6-sol at high effort, read-only, against `qt-updater-113` with #113 slice 3 uncommitted in the worktree. Five findings, all folded in:

1. Clearing and re-rendering the tray menu on refresh can delete the `QAction` a user just picked, because on macOS and DBusMenu the native menu closes before Qt delivers the activation. Decision 3 now updates the menu in place through a `sync()` from `render_menu`.
2. The "postpone while open" flag had no dependable close signal for a native or DBusMenu menu. Removed with finding 1. The suggested real StatusNotifierItem host test is declined, with the reason in the manual checks.
3. `supportsMessages()` cannot show a toast was seen. Decision 5 now defines True as submitted, as Tk's is, with the menu item as the durable announcement, and the manual check tests delivery apart from persistence. Codex also could not verify the draft's claim that Qt's Cocoa `showMessage` raises an uncatchable NSException outside a bundle (Qt 6.12 uses `NSUserNotificationCenter`, a different path from #45's), so that reason is replaced; macOS stays False.
4. Construction order: #113 slice 3 builds `QtUpdateHost` before the randomizing the tray's colours depend on. Decision 6 now states the order.
5. The About ctypes call needs exact prototypes (`objc_getClass` returns a pointer, not `int`), and a fake cannot catch a wrong one; Qt names the About item after the running bundle. Decision 8 now lists the prototypes, and the native call is run on the Mac in this issue.

Judged sound: decision 7's stop-before-release and restart-after-reclaim order, and decision 10's self-check rule against the shipped judge.

## Implementation reviews (Codex, 2026-10-13)

Slice 1 (6165ae3): a checkable tray item kept the mark Qt gave it on click when its action raised or left the model alone (fixed in a080b1c: the tray re-reads its getters after every action), and `deleteLater` after `app.exec()` returns is never processed (fixed in slice 2: `aboutToQuit` stops the tray, the `finally` stays as the backstop).

Slice 2 (07a457c): two findings kept as they are, because Tk behaves the same and decision 2 matches Tk. The Settings tray toggle reads a launch-time shadow while `QtUpdateHost.restore` reads `settings.json`, so the two can disagree if a replacement copy changed `tray_enabled` and then failed to take over (Tk: `__main__.py:381-389` against `TkHost.restore`). A tray whose `start()` fails after the probe passed keeps its Settings row, which then cannot bring an icon back (Tk: `TrayManager.start`). Both are worth revisiting in #142, when only one toolkit is left to change.
